# Workspace

Hanzo is one window with a rail down the left. Each icon on that rail is a room,
and a room is a whole product: a conversation, a coding run, the org's files.
You move between them the way you move between channels in a chat app — one
click, same window, nothing reloads.

That shape is deliberate. Three products people already used separately —
asking a model something, having one write software, keeping the files — sat in
three tabs with three sign-ins and three ideas of who you are. They are one
window now, over one identity, one balance and one API.

| Room | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Chat** | Ask anything. It searches, runs code, reads what you drop in, makes pictures. |
| **Dev** | Describe software and watch it get built, in your terminal or here. |
| **Drive** | The files your org keeps on Hanzo Cloud. |
| **Inbox** | What arrived, whichever network carried it. |
| **Agents** | What is running, what it did, and what it made. |
| **Tasks** | The work the org handed out, by where it stands. |

Chat and Dev are open to everyone. Inbox, Agents, Tasks and Drive are previews
and open per organization — see [Previews](#previews).

## Get in

Open [hanzo.ai/chat](https://hanzo.ai/chat).

**You do not need an account to start.** Chat answers a stranger on the free
model, and a free turn spends no balance. You are asked to consent when you
send, not when you arrive, because that is when the question of what happens to
your words actually arises.

Sign in to get everything else — your conversations kept, your org's rooms, your
balance, and any preview your org is in. Sign-in is [Hanzo IAM](https://hanzo.id).
One identity, and it can belong to several organizations.

<Callout title="An organization is the workspace">
Everything scoped — rooms, files, runs, billing — is scoped to an org, not to
you. If you belong to more than one, the header carries a switcher and the
whole window re-scopes in place. Your identity does not change; what you are
looking at does.
</Callout>

To bring somebody in, use **+ → Invite people**. That opens your org's own
people page at [hanzo.id](https://hanzo.id/account/organization), which is where
membership actually lives.

## The window

**The rail**, on the far left, is a column of rooms. At its head is your
organization's mark — that is identity, not a button. At its foot, three things
that do not scroll away:

- **+** — the Create menu. It becomes **✕** while open.
- **Theme** — Auto, Light or Dark.
- **Settings**.

**The sidebar** lists what is inside the room you are in: your channels and
direct messages, your recent conversations, your runs. `Find a conversation` at
its top filters that list. It is a filter over what is in front of you, not a
search across the org.

**The bar** across the top carries back and forward, your history, and the room
you are in. Back and forward are the browser's own — every room has an address,
so a room can be linked, bookmarked and shared.

**The composer** is at the bottom of every room, and it is the same component
everywhere. Whatever the room, you type into the same box.

## Create

The **+** at the foot of the rail is one menu, and every row does its verb:

| Row | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Message** | Starts a new conversation in Chat. |
| **Channel** | A group conversation by topic, in [Hanzo Team](https://hanzo.team). |
| **Huddle** | A call, at [meet.hanzo.ai](https://meet.hanzo.ai). |
| **Canvas** | Write something together, in Team. |
| **List** | Opens Tasks. |
| **Workflow** | Creates a workflow and opens it in [Auto](https://auto.hanzo.ai). |
| **Invite people** | Your org's people page at hanzo.id. |

A row that could not do its verb would not be here. Workflow really does create
one — it posts to `/v1/auto/flows` and takes you to it — rather than dropping
you on a landing page to start over.

## Chat

The composer is the page. Type and send.

Above it are four things a conversation can be about — **Chat**, **Dev**,
**News**, **Research** — and picking one changes what the same field is asking
for. It is one composer with four questions, not four products.

### Models

Hanzo serves **500+ models from 57 providers** through one endpoint, and the
model menu is that catalog. Anything in it can be named directly.

Or leave it on **Enso**, which chooses for you.

### Enso, and what it saves

Enso is three tiers over the whole field:

| Tier | For |
|---|---|
| `enso-flash` | The easy majority. |
| `enso` | The middle. |
| `enso-ultra` | The hard ones. Highest measured accuracy in our benchmark set. |

Most questions do not need a frontier model, and paying frontier rates for all
of them is where the money goes. Enso reads the request and routes it.

On our own numbers, with about one request in five escalating past Flash, that
is **69% less** than paying a top frontier rate on every request. If everything
stayed on Flash the ceiling is **83%**. Those figures come from the same
formula as the [calculator on the Enso page](https://hanzo.ai/enso), so the page
cannot quote a saving its own calculator will not produce.

<Callout type="warn" title="Not 90%">
A hard-coded 89% was published once and removed, because the model tops out at
83% and lands near 69% at a realistic mix. Quote 69%, or quote the calculator.
</Callout>

Enso is available only through Hanzo Cloud. The open-weight **Zen** family is
free to run here or anywhere else.

### What a conversation can reach for

Search the web, run the code it writes, read a file you drop in, make an image.
These are capabilities the model reaches for when the question calls for one,
and **Settings → Capabilities** is where you say which it may.

## Dev

Hanzo Dev writes software. It runs in three places, and it is the same agent in
all three.

**Terminal** — one line, in the repo you are already in:

```bash
curl -fsSL hanzo.sh | bash
```

**Editor** — any editor with a terminal, which is all of them.

**Here** — describe what to build and send. No install; the run starts on our
machines and opens in the room.

### A run

A run is the unit. It has a transcript you watch live, and four controls that
**steer** it: your command is recorded and the agent takes it between turns. It
does not interrupt work mid-flight, so a press is confirmed as *recorded* and
the feed reports what actually happened. A button that claimed the run was
paused would be lying for as long as the current turn takes.

Runs nest. Ask for something large and the agent fans out; **Agents** draws that
as a tree, because a list would hide the fan-out entirely.

For building an app visually in the browser — pages, preview, publish — use
[hanzo.app](https://hanzo.app).

## Drive

Drive is your organization's storage on Hanzo Cloud, read directly: buckets, and
the folders inside whichever is open.

It is not a second store with a friendlier name. A bucket is physically named
under your org's own prefix, so another tenant's files are not filtered out of
the answer — they are absent from it.

Drive is a preview.

## Inbox, Agents and Tasks

**Inbox** is what arrived, across whichever networks your org has connected. It
is inbound only, and it draws no composer, because a reply box that cannot reply
takes what someone typed and drops it.

**Agents** is the working view: what is running, what it did, what it made.

**Tasks** is the same work by where it stands, because **a task is a run**.
There is no parallel to-do store that would immediately disagree with the board
about the same work. The strip at the top hands work to an agent for real, waits
with you, and says which way it went.

## Previews

Chat and Dev are open to everyone. The other four rooms open when one of these
is true:

1. You are a platform SuperAdmin.
2. Your **organization** is in the alpha or beta cohort. That is a decision made
   on the cloud and asked of the cloud, so opening a room is a flag, not a
   release.
3. You are running Hanzo locally.

Signed out, still loading, or on any error, the answer is none of them: you get
Chat and Dev. It fails closed on purpose — a flags outage cannot leak an
unfinished room into the product.

A room you are entitled to but do not want can be hidden in Settings. Entitlement
and preference are two different questions, asked in two different places.

## Appearance

**Theme** at the foot of the rail: **Auto**, **Light** or **Dark**. Auto follows
your system. The glyph shows the choice you made, not the theme currently in
effect, so picking Auto does not look like picking Light on a light morning.

## Settings

Settings opens over the window. What is *not* in it is the point: keys, billing
and usage belong to the console, so those rows are marked as links and take you
there rather than growing a second home for one job.

| Section | |
|---|---|
| General | Appearance and language. |
| Account | [hanzo.id](https://hanzo.id) |
| Billing | [pay.hanzo.ai](https://pay.hanzo.ai) |
| Usage | [console.hanzo.ai/usage](https://console.hanzo.ai/usage) |
| API keys | [console.hanzo.ai/api-keys](https://console.hanzo.ai/api-keys) |
| Capabilities | What a model may reach for on your behalf. |
| Apps, Channels, Plugins, Skills | What this org has installed. |

A pane that is not built yet says so in one sentence. An honest empty pane is a
smaller lie than an invented one.

## Habits

The rooms are not four ways to do one thing. Each is the right home for
something:

- **A question** goes to Chat. Even a big one — it searches and runs code.
- **Work on a repo** goes to Dev, as a run. The transcript is the record.
- **Something the org keeps** goes to Drive, not into a conversation.
- **Something recurring** becomes a Workflow, once, instead of a question asked
  every week.
- **Something for people to talk about** goes to a channel in Team.

The one rule worth keeping: put shared work somewhere it has an address. Every
room, run and file here has one, and a thing with an address can be linked to.

## First day

1. Open [hanzo.ai/chat](https://hanzo.ai/chat) and ask something without signing in.
2. Sign in, so it is kept.
3. Check which org you are in; switch if you have more than one.
4. Send a question that needs the web, and one that needs code run.
5. Drop in a file and ask about it.
6. Leave the model on Enso for a day. Then look at your usage.
7. Open Dev and start a run — here, or install it in your terminal.
8. Steer a run mid-flight and watch what the feed reports.
9. Open **+** and make one Workflow.
10. Set your theme.
11. Open Settings → Capabilities and decide what a model may reach for.
12. Invite one person.
