**If you cannot replay a decision six months later and prove which weights, calibration table, and input state produced it, your AI system is not auditable.** Every Hanzo intelligence surface emits machine-verifiable digests.

## Reproducibility receipt anatomy

| Surface | Emitted provenance fields | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| **Kai Decisions** (`POST /v1/decisions`) | `state_hash` (`sha256:...`), `routing.checkpoint`, `routing.sha256`, `routing.calibration` (`cal_...`) | Exact input bytes, exact weight file, and exact temperature-calibration table |
| **Datasets** (`GET /v1/dataset/{name}/lineage`) | Materialized snapshot ID, upstream transforms, row digest | Exact rows evaluated or trained on |
| **Training Artifacts** (`GET /v1/train/artifacts/{sha256}`) | Content-addressed SHA-256 key verified by S3 on upload | Byte-identical dataset and checkpoint artifacts |
| **Audit Log** (`GET /v1/audit`) | Principal identity, org, timestamp, action digest | Who promoted, rolled back, or authorized each change |

## Calibration pinning in Decision Programs

Decision Programs pin `calibration` (`cal_...`) alongside `model: "kai"`. Because action thresholds (`>= 0.5`, `>= 0.85`) hold only for the calibration table they were validated against, running under a different calibration automatically demotes an `enforced` program to `shadow` until re-validated.
