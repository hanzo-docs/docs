**A `noul` returns the probability that a statement about the state is true.**

## Ask

```bash
curl -sS https://api.hanzo.ai/v1/decisions \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $HANZO_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "model": "kai",
    "state": {"message": "The app crashes every time I open the statements tab."},
    "questions": {
      "app_error": {"type": "noul", "instructions": "`message` reports that the app is not working.",
                    "criteria": {"true": "the app shows an error, crashes or refuses an action",
                                 "false": "the app works, or the message is about something else"}}
    }
  }' | jq '.answers'
```

What `api.hanzo.ai` answered on 2026-09-28:

{/* out: sh/noul.sh */}
```json
{
  "app_error": {
    "type": "noul",
    "noul": 0.6077,
    "answer_confidence": 0.6077
  }
}
```

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `noul` | P(true), from 0 to 1. |
| `answer_confidence` | The probability of the likelier side, max(p, 1 − p). |
| `confidence` | \|2p − 1\|: the choice formula (n·p_max − 1)/(n − 1) with n = 2, so 0 at even odds and 1 at certainty. |

The statement leans true, and only just. The message states a crash outright; a
person would not hesitate. The measurement below shows how often this happens.

## How Kai reads a noul

A noul is two options scored against the question and the state: `false:`
followed by the `false` criterion, and `true:` followed by the `true` one.
Without `criteria`, the sides read "no, the statement does not hold" and "yes,
the statement holds". P(true) is σ(s(true) − s(false)), so both sides are scored
by the same function. `criteria` takes the keys `true` and `false`, either or
both; any other key is refused.

## Phrasing, measured

Eight messages to a bank's support desk, four about a failing app and four
about something else, asked whether the app is failing in four forms: a bare
question, a statement with both sides described, a yes/no `choice`, and a
five-way `choice` in which the failing app is one label among the things a
message can be about.

```python
messages = [
    (True, "My new card came yesterday but the app will not let me activate it. It keeps saying the code is invalid."),
    (True, "The app crashes every time I open the statements tab."),
    (True, "I tried to send money three times and the app just shows a spinning wheel and then an error."),
    (True, "Since the last update I cannot log in to the app, it says something went wrong."),
    (False, "How long does a transfer to a German bank account usually take?"),
    (False, "I lost my card on the train this morning, please block it."),
    (False, "Can I raise my daily ATM withdrawal limit?"),
    (False, "Thanks, the refund arrived today. Great service!"),
]
broken = "the app shows an error, crashes or refuses an action"
working = "the app works, or the message is about something else"
forms = {
    "question": Noul(instructions="Is the app broken?"),
    "statement": Noul(
        instructions="`message` reports that the app is not working.", criteria={"true": broken, "false": working}
    ),
    "yes/no": Choice(
        instructions="Does `message` report that the app is not working?", criteria={"yes": broken, "no": working}
    ),
    "five-way": Choice(
        instructions="What does `message` report?",
        criteria={
            "app_error": broken,
            "card": "a card is lost, stolen, blocked or needs activating",
            "transfer": "sending or receiving money",
            "limits": "limits, fees or account settings",
            "other": "thanks, feedback or anything else",
        },
    ),
}
```

P(the app is failing) by form:

{/* out: python/phrasing.py */}
```text
truth   question  statement     yes/no   five-way
True        0.56       0.50       0.66       0.04
True        0.52       0.61       0.96       0.95
True        0.36       0.64       0.99       0.99
True        0.52       0.55       0.74       0.64
False       0.00       0.64       0.47       0.02
False       0.47       0.49       0.65       0.00
False       0.14       0.44       0.52       0.06
False       0.19       0.54       0.73       0.16
right        7/8        5/8        5/8        7/8
```

- **The bare question** is right on seven, but three of its true answers sit
  between 0.52 and 0.56: a small shift in wording could flip them.
- **The statement with both sides described** never leaves 0.44 to 0.64,
  true or false: on this statement it barely tells the cases apart.
- **The yes/no `choice`** calls three of the four false messages true.
- **The five-way `choice`** separates the cases widely and misses one: it files
  the card that the app will not activate under `card`, which also fits.

So on this checkpoint:

- Write a noul as a statement and describe both sides in `criteria`, so each
  answer has a stated meaning. On everyday questions a bare question can come
  back inverted.
- When the statement is one of several things a case can be about, ask a
  `choice` that names them all and read the probability of the one you need.
- Check a noul on cases whose answer you know before you threshold it. Values
  that stay near 0.5 for true and false cases alike mean the question is not
  being read; rephrase it or change its form.

## Act on it

Two thresholds split a noul three ways: act on yes above the upper one, on no
below the lower one, and hand the middle to a person or a rule. Where they sit
depends on what a wrong yes and a wrong no each cost you, measured on your own
cases: [Confidence and thresholds](/docs/kai/confidence).
