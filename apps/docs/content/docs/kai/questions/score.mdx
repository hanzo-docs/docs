**A `score` returns where the state sits on levels you define, lowest first,
with a probability for each level.**

## Ask

```bash
curl -sS https://api.hanzo.ai/v1/decisions \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $HANZO_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "model": "kai",
    "state": {"review": "The blender is loud and the lid cracked after a month, but it still makes a decent smoothie."},
    "questions": {
      "sentiment": {"type": "score", "instructions": "How positive is `review`?",
                    "criteria": ["very negative", "negative", "neutral", "positive", "very positive"]}
    }
  }' | jq '.answers'
```

What `api.hanzo.ai` answered on 2026-09-28:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `score` | The expected level, Σ i·pᵢ, from 0 for the first level to n − 1 for the last. It can fall between levels. |
| `probabilities` | One probability per level, keyed by the level's index as a string, `"0"` first; they sum to 1. The Python SDK keys them by integer. |
| `legend` | Each index with its level as you sent it. |
| `answer_confidence` | The probability of the most probable level. |
| `confidence` | (n·p_max − 1)/(n − 1) over n levels. |

The review is mixed, and Kai splits it between neutral and positive. Its two
readings disagree: the expected level, 2.33, rounds to neutral, while the most
probable level is positive at 0.39. Branch on the most probable level and its
`answer_confidence`; rank many states by `score`.

## Levels

- **A list, lowest first.** Each level is text, an object or an array. A `null`
  level is refused: every level needs words.
- **Read one at a time.** Kai reads each level as its own text, `level i: …`,
  and scores it against the question and the state. `score` is only as ordered as
  your list.
- **One dimension.** A level that mixes two things ("fast and friendly") has no
  place for a state that is one and not the other; ask two scores and combine
  them in code.

## Measured: how levels are worded

Six reviews a person would place from very negative to very positive, scored
against short level names and against one-sentence levels:

```python
from hanzo_kai import Kai, Score

reviews = [
    ("very negative", "Arrived broken, support never answered, and the refund took six weeks. Avoid."),
    ("negative", "Not worth the price. It works, but the motor already sounds tired."),
    ("neutral", "It is fine. Nothing special, nothing wrong."),
    ("mixed", "The blender is loud and the lid cracked after a month, but it still makes a decent smoothie."),
    ("positive", "Does exactly what it says. Quiet, fast, easy to clean. I would buy it again."),
    ("very positive", "Best purchase this year. My kids love it and I use it every single morning!"),
]
questions = {
    "names": Score(
        instructions="How positive is `review`?",
        criteria=["very negative", "negative", "neutral", "positive", "very positive"],
    ),
    "sentences": Score(
        instructions="How does the writer of `review` feel about the product?",
        criteria=[
            "Very negative: angry, would warn others away.",
            "Negative: disappointed, more bad than good.",
            "Mixed or neutral: good and bad balance out, or no opinion.",
            "Positive: satisfied, more good than bad.",
            "Very positive: delighted, would recommend.",
        ],
    ),
}

with Kai() as kai:
    print("a person says   level names   sentences")
    for label, text in reviews:
        d = kai.decide(state={"review": text}, questions=questions)
        names, sentences = d.scores["names"], d.scores["sentences"]
        print(f"{label:15} {names.score:11.2f}   {sentences.score:9.2f}")
```

{/* out: python/levels.py */}
```text
a person says   level names   sentences
very negative          1.49        1.62
negative               1.51        1.89
neutral                1.47        1.69
mixed                  2.33        2.64
positive               2.42        2.13
very positive          3.15        2.37
```

Neither wording orders all six. With level names, the three reviews a person
rates neutral or lower all score under 1.6 and the three rated mixed or higher
all score over 2.3, though the lower three are out of order among themselves.
With sentences, the mixed review outscores both positive ones. Every score
stays between 1.4 and 3.2 of the 0 to 4 range: on a scale of your own, Kai
rarely commits to the ends. Test your levels on states whose level you know
before you act on them; [Limits](/docs/kai/limits) has more of what is known.
