title: Confidence and thresholds
description: Tell a probability from confidence and answer_confidence, read what a spread answer means, and set the threshold below which your code escalates.

**Every answer carries its own uncertainty. Code acts on the answers above a
threshold it has tested, and hands the rest to a person or a rule.**

## Three numbers

| Field | On | Is | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| `probabilities`, `noul` | choice and score; noul | the probability of each option; P(true) | 0 to 1 |
| `answer_confidence` | every answer | the probability of the reported answer, p_max; for a noul, max(p, 1 − p) | 1/n to 1 |
| `confidence` | every answer | (n·p_max − 1)/(n − 1) over n options; for a noul, \|2p − 1\| | 0 to 1 |

`answer_confidence` is a probability, so it can be checked against how often
such answers turn out right. `confidence` rescales it so that 0 means no better
than a uniform guess, whatever the number of options: a top probability of 0.5
is confidence 0 for two labels and 0.375 for five. One threshold on `confidence`
therefore means the same for a yes/no question and a thirty-way choice.

## A clear case and an unclear one

One question, two messages, and a desk that acts at confidence 0.6:

```python
from hanzo_kai import Kai, Choice

need = Choice(
    instructions="What does `message` need from IT?",
    criteria={
        "password": "reset a password or unlock an account",
        "access": "permission to a system, folder or app",
        "hardware": "a broken or missing laptop, monitor or phone",
        "network": "Wi-Fi, VPN or internet problems",
        "other": "anything else",
    },
)
messages = [
    "I got locked out after too many attempts, can you unlock my account?",
    "Since the Wi-Fi password changed I cannot open the shared drive.",
]
THRESHOLD = 0.6  # the desk acts on answers at or above this confidence

with Kai() as kai:
    for text in messages:
        a = kai.decide(state={"message": text}, questions={"need": need}).choices["need"]
        top = sorted(a.probabilities.items(), key=lambda kv: -kv[1])[:3]
        action = f"route to {a.choice}" if a.confidence >= THRESHOLD else "ask a person"
        print(text)
        print(f"  top three {[(label, round(p, 4)) for label, p in top]}")
        print(f"  answer_confidence {a.answer_confidence:.4f}  confidence {a.confidence:.4f}  -> {action}")
```

What `api.hanzo.ai` answered on 2026-09-28:

{/* out: python/confidence.py */}
```text

  top three [('password', 0.9999), ('access', 0.0), ('hardware', 0.0)]
  answer_confidence 0.9999  confidence 0.9999  -> route to password
Since the Wi-Fi password changed I cannot open the shared drive.
  top three [('access', 0.3756), ('password', 0.3261), ('hardware', 0.1637)]
  answer_confidence 0.3756  confidence 0.2195  -> ask a person
```

The lockout is plain, and the desk acts. The Wi-Fi message touches several
labels at once, and Kai spreads its answer across them instead of picking one
with false certainty. Its confidence is under the line, so a person decides.

## Reading a spread

- **Two labels close at the top**: the case fits both. It may really be about
  both; handle both, or ask.
- **Probability across many labels**: no label fits, or the state lacks what
  decides. Add a label, or send the part of the case that settles it.
- **A score split between neighbouring levels**: the state sits between them.
  Split between distant levels, the scale mixes two things, or the case is
  ambiguous.

## Set the threshold from your outcomes

1. Take a few hundred cases from your own traffic, each with the answer a person
   gave.
2. Ask Kai the questions you will ship, worded as you will ship them.
3. For each candidate threshold, measure how often the answers at or above it are
   right, and what share of cases they cover.
4. Pick the lowest threshold whose accuracy covers the cost of acting wrongly.
   Below it, escalate.
5. Measure again when `routing.checkpoint` or `routing.calibration` changes, or
   when you reword a question.

A threshold scales with what the action costs: routing a ticket to a queue can
act on less confidence than unlocking an account.

Kai's probabilities were calibrated on held-out data from its training suites,
and per-suite calibration error is published with its
[harness results](https://github.com/hanzoai/benchmarks/tree/c61361fedc5882b0d83b2f5d5a6a35c56ffeb3c3/decision/results/kai-a7).
On questions unlike those suites it can be confident and wrong: one wording of a
routing question sent a request to write a note to `code` at 0.67
([Choice questions](/docs/kai/questions/choice#measured-wording-moves-answers)).
That is why the threshold comes from your outcomes, not from the number alone.
