**A moderation decision needs a third outcome besides remove and keep: not
sure, so hold it for a person.** Code sets it from the answer's confidence,
and the threshold trades automation for agreement. On 80 labelled posts from
the harness's toxicity suite the trade depended on how the question was asked:
as a yes/no choice, holding the least confident quarter barely moved agreement
(0.700 to 0.717); as the noul the suite trains, it held three posts, all three
of them wrong answers.

## The idea

A choice answer carries a `confidence`, (n·p_max − 1)/(n − 1): 0 when the
labels are equally likely, 1 when one takes all the probability. For two
labels that is 2·p_max − 1, the same number as |2p − 1| on a noul's P(yes).
Below a threshold, the post is held:

```python
def outcome(yes: float, confidence: float, threshold: float) -> str:
    """remove or keep when the confidence clears the threshold, else uncertain."""
    if confidence < threshold:
        return "uncertain"
    return "remove" if yes >= 0.5 else "keep"
```

There is another way to get a third outcome: offer it as a label and let Kai
pick it. The script measures that too, and asks the toxicity question in the
harness's words three ways, all in the same call per post:

```python
ASK = "Is `post` toxic: rude, disrespectful or likely to make someone leave the discussion?"  # the harness's words
YESNO = {"yes": "it is toxic", "no": "it is not toxic"}
QUESTIONS = {
    "moderate": Choice(instructions=ASK, criteria=YESNO),
    "unsure": Choice(instructions=ASK, criteria=YESNO | {"unsure": "it could be read either way"}),
    "noul": Noul(instructions=ASK),
    "labels": Choice(
        instructions="How should a moderator label `post`?",
        criteria={
            "acceptable": "polite, neutral or merely blunt",
            "toxic": "rude, disrespectful or likely to make someone leave the discussion",
        },
    ),
}
```

The 80 posts are drawn with a fixed seed from `app.moderation_toxicity`; the
labels are the suite's. The whole script is
[`choices.py`](https://github.com/hanzoai/python-sdk/blob/main/pkg/hanzo-kai/examples/choices.py).

## What the numbers say

- **How the question is asked matters more than the threshold.** The same
  words as the noul the suite trains agreed on 67 of 80. As a yes/no choice,
  56. Relabelled as acceptable or toxic, 35: worse than a coin, on two
  balanced labels.
- **On the yes/no choice, holding posts buys almost nothing.** Holding
  everything under 0.8 automates 60 of 80, and agreement moves from 0.700 to
  0.717: the choice's wrong answers are about as confident as its right ones.
- **On the noul, the hold works as intended.** At 0.6 it holds three posts, and
  all three were wrong answers: agreement on the rest rises from 0.838 to 0.870.
  Above 0.6 nothing more is held, so on this suite a band cannot trade further.
- **An `unsure` label is not a substitute.** Kai picked it for 11 posts, and
  the other 69 agree with the labels less often (0.681) than the choice without
  it (0.700).
- **On the whole suite, Kai's noul is ahead of Jev**: 0.802 against 0.662 over
  all 400 posts
  ([benchmark results](https://github.com/hanzoai/benchmarks/tree/c61361fedc5882b0d83b2f5d5a6a35c56ffeb3c3/decision/results/kai-a7)).

## Adapt it

- Ask a question in the shape and words it was measured in when you have them,
  and put the uncertain outcome in code on top, as `outcome` does.
- Pick the threshold from a table like this on posts your moderators have
  labelled: the share automated against agreement on what was automated.
- Route held posts to a person with the probability attached, and feed their
  decisions back as labels for the next threshold.
- Measure new wording before you use it for a question the checkpoint knows
  in other words: here, new labels and a new sentence agreed on 32 fewer posts
  than the noul.
