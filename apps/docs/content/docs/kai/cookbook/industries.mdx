**Report the fine label when Kai is sure of it, the group above it when only
the group is clear, and nothing when neither is.** The group's probability is
the sum of its labels' probabilities, so the fallback costs no extra question.
On 60 Banking77 messages the top label was right 53 times and its group 57
times, but Kai was so confident that the fallback only engaged at a label
threshold of 0.99 and above.

## The idea

Banking77 has 77 intents, and several differ by a hair: `card arrival` and
`card delivery estimate`, `exchange rate` and `card payment wrong exchange
rate`. Many applications can act on a coarser answer ("a card question", "a
currency question") when the fine one is uncertain. The script groups the 77
labels into nine groups of our own:

| Group | Labels | For example |
|---|---|---|
| card | 12 | card arrival, card delivery estimate, activate my card |
| broken card | 6 | card not working, contactless not working |
| security | 6 | lost or stolen card, pin blocked |
| payment | 10 | extra charge on statement, pending card payment |
| cash | 7 | pending cash withdrawal, atm support |
| transfer | 11 | transfer timing, failed transfer |
| top up | 11 | automatic top up, top up failed |
| currency | 6 | exchange rate, fiat currency support |
| account | 8 | verify my identity, terminate account |

One call per message asks the harness's own 77-way question. Code then decides
the level: the label if its confidence clears a threshold, else the most
probable group if that group's summed probability is at least 0.8, else
review.

## The code

```python
def place(answer, fine: float, coarse: float = 0.8) -> tuple[str, str | None]:
    """The level Kai can answer at, and the answer: a label, its group, or nothing for review."""
    if answer.confidence >= fine:
        return "label", answer.choice
    mass = Counter()
    for label, p in answer.probabilities.items():
        mass[PARENT[label]] += p
    group, p = mass.most_common(1)[0]
    return ("group", group) if p >= coarse else ("review", None)
```

`PARENT` maps each label to its group. The script, with the groups and a draw
of six messages for each of the harness's ten gold labels, is
[`industries.py`](https://github.com/hanzoai/python-sdk/blob/main/pkg/hanzo-kai/examples/industries.py).

## What the numbers say

- **Kai's confidence on this suite sits near 1.** Only two answers fell under
  0.9, both wrong, and both went to review because neither group reached 0.8
  either. Up to a label threshold of 0.9, no answer falls back to a group.
- **At 0.9999 the fallback does its job.** 47 answers stay labels (46 right), 11
  fall back to their group (10 right), 2 go to review: 56 of the 58 answered
  are right, against 53 of 60 with no fallback.
- **Four of the seven wrong labels are in the right group**, and three of those
  four are at 0.999 or above. `card arrival` against `card delivery estimate` is
  a split Banking77 draws finely: "How do I know when my card will arrive?" is
  labelled arrival, and Kai said estimate at 1.0000.
- **The group of the top label alone** was right for 57 of 60. When a group is
  all you act on, report it and skip the thresholds.
- **The threshold that works here was found by sweeping it on labelled data.**
  With probabilities this close to 1, pick it from a sweep like the table's on
  your own labelled messages, not by feel.

## Adapt it

- Define groups by what your application does next, not by how labels are
  spelled: one group per queue, team or reply.
- Sum probabilities up the tree for any depth; a node's probability is the sum
  of its children's.
- Run the sweep on your own labelled messages and pick the pair of thresholds
  whose coverage and accuracy you can live with at each level.
