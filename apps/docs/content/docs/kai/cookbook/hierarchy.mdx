title: Classify down a tree
description: "Classify into a label tree with a beam over Kai's choice probabilities, parent first, then children, and measure it against one flat choice at each level."
---

**A tree classifier asks for the parent first, keeps the likeliest few, then
asks for a child under each and multiplies the probabilities.** On 50 MASSIVE
utterances with gold labels, the tree was right on 42 intents and the flat
choice over all 59 on 44. The tree lost on the parent question: for 6
utterances the right scenario was not among its two best.

## The idea

MASSIVE files every intent under a scenario, and its names say which:
`alarm_set` sits under `alarm`, `iot_cleaning` under `iot`. The script builds
the tree from the harness's `massive.en` suite (59 intents under 18 scenarios)
and classifies each utterance two ways:

- **Flat**: one choice over all 59 intents.
- **Tree, beam of two**: one choice over the 18 scenarios, then, for the two
  most probable, one choice over each one's intents. A path's probability is
  P(scenario) × P(intent | scenario), and the best path wins. A scenario with a
  single intent needs no second question.

Two calls per utterance: the first carries the scenario question and the flat
choice together, the second carries the kept scenarios' intent questions. A
scenario's description is its intents' descriptions joined, taken as they are
from the harness.

## The code

```python
ASK = "What is the user asking for in `utterance`?"
SCENARIO = Choice(
    instructions="Which part of a voice assistant is `utterance` for?",
    criteria={scenario: ", ".join(DESCRIBE[i] for i in intents) for scenario, intents in TREE.items()},
)
INTENT = {
    s: Choice(instructions=ASK, criteria={i: DESCRIBE[i] for i in intents})
    for s, intents in TREE.items()
    if len(intents) > 1
}
FLAT = Choice(instructions=ASK, criteria=DESCRIBE)


def classify(kai: Kai, state: dict) -> tuple[str, str, list[str], int]:
    """The flat answer, the tree's answer, the scenarios kept, and how many second-level questions it took."""
    first = kai.decide(state=state, questions={"scenario": SCENARIO, "flat": FLAT}).choices
    beam = sorted(first["scenario"].probabilities.items(), key=lambda kv: -kv[1])[:WIDTH]
    asks = {scenario: INTENT[scenario] for scenario, _ in beam if scenario in INTENT}
    second = kai.decide(state=state, questions=asks).choices if asks else {}
    paths = [
        (p * (second[scenario].probabilities[intent] if scenario in second else 1.0), intent)
        for scenario, p in beam
        for intent in TREE[scenario]
    ]
    return first["flat"].choice, max(paths)[1], [scenario for scenario, _ in beam], len(asks)
```

## What the numbers say

- **The flat choice won at both levels**: 44 intents right against 42, and 46
  scenarios against 43.
- **The tree's losses come from its first question.** The right scenario was
  outside the beam for 6 of 50 utterances, and nothing below can recover a
  scenario the beam dropped. Four of the tree's misses are the same mistake:
  "turn on the vacuum cleaner" and "enable my plug" landed on
  `play_audiobook`, where the flat choice had them right.
- **The parent descriptions are the likely cause, not measured here.** The
  `iot` scenario is described by its intents' names joined: "iot cleaning, iot
  coffee, iot hue lightchange, …, iot wemo on". Nothing in that says vacuum or
  plug to a reader, and the scenario question read it the same way.
- **The tree fixed two of the flat choice's misses**, "what's sensational in
  time of india" and "i want the status on my screen brightness", by
  committing to a scenario first.
- **One gold label is arguable.** MASSIVE files "what is the exchange rate of
  us dollar to pound sterling" under `news_query`; both methods said
  `qa_currency`.

## Adapt it

- Try the flat choice first. A tree earns its extra call when the flat label
  set is too long to describe well, or when your labels nest by meaning, not by
  a naming convention.
- Describe each parent in the words users say ("robot vacuum, smart plugs,
  lights, the coffee machine"), and measure the parent question alone: its
  "gold inside the beam" rate caps the whole tree.
- Widen the beam where the parent is often uncertain; each kept parent adds
  one question to the second call, not a call.
- Deeper trees repeat the second step per level, one call per level.
