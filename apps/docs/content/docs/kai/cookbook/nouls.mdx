---
title: Band a yes or no
description: "Act on a yes/no answer only when its probability is near 0 or 1, send the middle band to review with the probability on the record, and measure what each band costs in automation and buys in accuracy."
---

**A noul's answer is a probability, so the decision rule is two numbers: act
above the high one, act the other way below the low one, and send what falls
between to a person.** On 80 labelled emails from the harness's phishing suite,
Kai answered every one near 0 or 1, so every band automated all 80, with 78
right. Both misses were confident, and no band would have caught them.

## The idea

A yes/no answer is P(yes). Rounding it at 0.5 throws away what the model says
about its own certainty; a band keeps it. The record of each decision carries
the raw probability beside the route, so a reviewer, an audit or a later
threshold change can use it.

The script asks the harness's own question, word for word, about 80 emails
drawn with a fixed seed from `app.phishing`:
"Is this email a phishing or scam attempt to steal money, credentials, or
personal data?", with criteria for both sides. It then routes every answer
under five bands, from none (act on everything at 0.5) to (0.01, 0.99), and
counts what each automates and how often the automated answer matches the
label.

## The code

```python
def route(p: float, low: float, high: float) -> str:
    """Where an email goes: automatic at either end of the band, a person in between."""
    return "phishing" if p >= high else "legitimate" if p <= low else "review"
```

```python
        p = ask(kai, state["email"], questions)  # the harness's own question
        records.append({"case": index, "p": p, "route": route(p, 0.1, 0.9), "gold": gold[NAME]["idx"] == 1})
```

Kai reads 1,024 tokens per question on this checkpoint. If the API refuses an
email as longer than that (`422`, `state_too_long`), `ask` halves the email and
asks again. The whole script is

## What the numbers say

- **Kai left nothing in the middle.** All 80 probabilities are under 0.05 or at
  0.95 and above, so every band from none to (0.01, 0.99) automates all 80 and
  gets 78 right. On this suite a band costs nothing and buys nothing.
- **Both misses are confident.** Case 49 is an email whose whole text is the
  word "empty", labelled legitimate; Kai gave it 0.9994. Case 68 is a paragraph
  of aphorisms the dataset labels phishing; Kai gave it 0.0009. A band catches
  uncertain answers, never confident wrong ones.
- **On the whole suite Kai is more accurate than Jev here**: 0.990 against
  0.900 over all 400 emails
  ([benchmark results](https://github.com/hanzoai/benchmarks/tree/c61361fedc5882b0d83b2f5d5a6a35c56ffeb3c3/decision/results/kai-a7)).
  Its probabilities sit at the ends on this question; on a question where they
  spread, the same band trades automation for accuracy, as
  [An uncertain outcome](/docs/kai/cookbook/choices) shows on toxicity.

## Adapt it

- Keep `p` on every record, automated or not. It is what lets you move a band
  later, and what an auditor needs to see.
- Set the band from a table like this on your own labelled traffic: the share
  automated against the accuracy of what was automated.
- Sample a few automated answers for a person too. The confident misses above
  are only found that way.
- Measure a yes/no question of your own on labelled cases before it gates
  anything. The phishing question is one the harness trains; on a new one,
  [Known limits](/docs/kai/limits#yes-or-no-questions) found a choice whose
  labels name the outcomes more reliable than a noul.
