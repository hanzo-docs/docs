**Code finds the dates, Kai says which one answers, code does the arithmetic.**
Over nine messages of ours, the five answers the script acted on were all
right, including one that had to count 30 days from a date named elsewhere in
the message; four went to review, and one of those was wrong.

## The idea

A message about a payment names several dates: when the invoice was issued,
when goods ship, when payment is due. Some are calendar dates, some are relative
("30 days after the invoice date", "yesterday", "by Friday"). The work splits
three ways:

1. **Code finds candidates.** Regular expressions match calendar dates and a
   small set of relative forms.
2. **Kai checks each candidate.** For each one the script asks whether the
   message implies "The payment due date is 30 days after the invoice date.",
   and keeps the candidate with the highest probability of entailment. That is
   the premise-and-hypothesis question [Check citations](/docs/kai/cookbook/citations)
   uses. When the winning expression counts from a named event ("the invoice
   date", "the kickoff"), a second round of checks asks which calendar date
   that event is.
3. **Code resolves.** Counting days, finding the next Friday and fixing the
   year are arithmetic, and code does them against the day the message was
   sent.

An answer is automatic when every check it rests on reached 0.7; below 0.3 the
answer is that the message does not give the date; anything between goes to a
person with what Kai supported.

## The code

```python
def best(kai: Kai, text: str, hypotheses: dict[str, str]) -> tuple[str, float]:
    """The key whose hypothesis the text supports most, with its P(entailment): one check per hypothesis."""
    support = {
        key: kai.decide(state={"premise": text, "hypothesis": hypothesis}, questions={"check": CHECK})
        .choices["check"]
        .probabilities["entailment"]
        for key, hypothesis in hypotheses.items()
    }
    return max(support.items(), key=lambda item: item[1])
```

`CHECK` is the three-way relation question, `spans` runs the regular
expressions, `phrase` extends "30 days after" to the end of its clause, and
`resolve` does the calendar arithmetic. They and the nine labelled messages are
in [`dates.py`](https://github.com/hanzoai/python-sdk/blob/main/pkg/hanzo-kai/examples/dates.py).

## What the numbers say

- **Every automatic answer was right.** That includes the due date, where Kai
  picked "30 days after the invoice date" over both calendar dates and then
  matched "the invoice date" to February 27, and the delivery date, which the
  message does not give: its best candidate, the payment date, reached only
  0.21.
- **Review caught the one wrong answer.** For the warranty, Kai preferred the
  purchase date (0.39) to "90 days from purchase"; under 0.7, it went to a
  person.
- **Three reviews carry the right date.** The kickoff moved from March 10 to
  March 13, and Kai matched it to the new date, at 0.57. The trial and the
  Friday deadline were right at 0.68 and 0.65. On these nine messages a line
  at 0.5 would have automated all three and still held the warranty; nine
  messages are too few to set a line by, so set yours on labelled messages of
  your own.
- **One choice over all the expressions does worse.** Asked which expression
  gives the date as a single choice, Kai tends to pick a calendar date over the
  relative expression that answers. [Extract values](/docs/kai/cookbook/values)
  measures a single choice against a check per candidate.

## Adapt it

- Add the relative forms your messages use to `RELATIVE`, with their arithmetic
  in `resolve`. What Kai checks stays the same.
- Resolve in the sender's time zone and calendar; business days, holidays and
  "end of month" are rules for code, not questions for Kai.
- Keep the evidence with the answer (`why` in the script), so the person
  reviewing sees which expression and which anchor Kai supported.
